Abstract

While image captioning has gained significant attention, the potential of captioning time-series images, prevalent in areas like finance and healthcare, remains largely untapped. Existing time-series captioning methods typically offer generic, domain-agnostic descriptions of time-series shapes and struggle to adapt to new domains without substantial retraining. To address these limitations, we introduce TADACap, a retrieval-based framework to generate domain-aware captions for time-series images, capable of adapting to new domains without retraining. Building on TADACap, we propose a novel retrieval strategy that retrieves diverse image-caption pairs from a target domain database, namely TADACap-diverse. We benchmarked TADACap-diverse against state-of-the-art methods and ablation variants. TADACap-diverse demonstrates comparable semantic accuracy while requiring significantly less annotation effort.

Core contributions

  • 1A new taskDomain-aware captioning of time series charts, beyond generic shape descriptions.
  • 2No retrainingOff-the-shelf models plus in-context examples adapt TADACap to any new domain.
  • 3Diverse retrievalA determinantal point process picks a few examples that cover the domain, instead of nearest neighbours.
  • 4Four annotationsOnly the k = 4 selected charts need captions, not the whole database.
  • 5New datasetsTwo real (COVID cases, knee angles) and two synthetic (stocks, physics) captioned datasets.
  • 6Strong semantic accuracyMatches nearest-neighbour retrieval on SPICE, and beats Gemini-V and mPLUG-Owl.

Method

1Why domain matters

The same curve means different things in different fields: to a trader it may be a price spike followed by volatility; to a doctor, blood sugar rising after a meal. Existing time series captioners describe only the shape, and adapting image captioners to a new domain usually needs retraining.

2TADACap

A domain-agnostic captioner describes the shape of the query chart. For each retrieved example, we pair its generic description with its in-domain caption. An LLM (GPT-4 or Vicuna) sees these pairs as “translation” examples and translates the query’s generic description into the target domain.

3Diverse retrieval

Retrieval methods usually need a fully captioned database to find nearest neighbours, but captioned time series barely exist. TADACap-diverse instead embeds every chart with CLIP and uses a determinantal point process to select the k most diverse charts. Users then caption only those, a large saving in annotation effort.

Results

  • Beats multimodal LLMs: with GPT-4, TADACap-diverse outperforms Gemini-V and mPLUG-Owl on SPICE on all four datasets.
  • Same accuracy, far fewer labels: it matches TADACap-nn (similar to the state-of-the-art SmallCap) on semantic accuracy while captioning only 4 charts.
  • Diversity beats random: diverse examples usually do better than random ones, and much better than no examples.
